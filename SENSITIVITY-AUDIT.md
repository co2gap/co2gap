# Paired sensitivity: frozen baseline and sampling stability

Audit date: 30 August 2026. Release under study: **2026-09-01**.
This is an experimental diagnostic, not a release revision, a confidence
interval or preparation of the January release. All input data remain frozen;
private samples and calculation outputs stay outside git.

## What this audit checks

The nine scenarios in `uncertainty-scenarios.json` comprise the nominal and
eight one-factor alternatives: load factor 0.72/0.92 instead of 0.82; carried
reserve 1,000/3,000 kg instead of 2,000 kg; baseline altitude minus/plus 1,000 ft;
ground speed instead of IAS-preferred airspeed; and ground definition
`a1000t70` instead of `a3000t70`. They are declared steps, not quantiles of a
parameter distribution. Real, ideal and hybrid use the same scenario
parameters; altitude and ground-definition scenarios change only the relevant
part of that coupled calculation.

Two additional sample seeds were run after inspecting the first result. This
is an explicitly **post-run sampling-stability diagnostic**, not a
pre-registered confirmatory analysis. It does not change the separate blinded
external-validation sample, matching protocol, minimum counts or outcomes.

## Reference reconstruction: a real harness defect

The first runner reselected the nominal cruise altitude. The historical
optimiser caches by type and rounded 50 km bucket while calculating the cached
value at the first flight's exact distance. A sample changes that processing
history. Consequently, a zero perturbation could change the reference before
the experiment began. On the third initial sample the largest differences were
919.082 kg CO2 ideal and 2,272.287 kg CO2 hybrid per flight. Weighted aggregate
differences were much smaller, so inspecting only headline closure would hide
the problem.

The historical cache defect was already documented in the separate scie/NOx
work on 23 August. The new finding is its effect on this sensitivity harness.
An isolated case selected 24,000 ft instead of the stored 22,000 ft; passing the
stored altitude reconstructed both baselines exactly, with either one or two
wind days. The runner now anchors every scenario to stored `cruise_alt_ft` and
applies the declared offset. It does not replace the historical optimiser.

A nominal mismatch now raises an error before aggregation, with a roundoff
tolerance of `1e-10` relative or `1e-6 kg` absolute. It is not handled by dropping
the flight. The report still carries maximum per-flight and weighted closure
diagnostics.

Ceiling clipping was an initial hypothesis, not the cause. A scan of all
1,833,127 frozen rows (190 distinct type/altitude pairs) found **zero** that
would be clipped at either nominal or plus 1,000 ft. The experimental altitude
hook now expresses the finite-difference step exactly, with a 1,000 ft floor;
its scenarios are not operationally feasible flight-plan recommendations.

## Sampling design and interpretation

Each draw contains 2,549 flights in 534 aircraft-type, distance and coverage
strata, taking up to five per stratum. Inverse inclusion weights expand to
1,833,127 flights, but **do not reproduce the exact population headline**.
Maximum weight is 13,524.6 and Kish effective sample size is 341.945. That
weight-based diagnostic is not an outcome-specific standard error.

Seeds are 20260901, 20260902 and 20260903. Together they contain 6,921 distinct
flights; pairwise overlaps are 284, 321 and 304 flights, with 183 shared by all
three. The runs must not be described as 7,647 distinct observations. The first
sample was regenerated byte-for-byte before use.

## Outcome: two accepted replications, one rejected reference

Both first replications processed all 2,549 requested flights with zero model
failures and verified source manifests. Nominal ideal and hybrid reconstruction
is **exactly zero**, both at the maximum per-flight difference and in weighted
aggregates. Their expanded nominal estimates are:

| Lateral gap (%) | 7.466466 | 7.606884 | 7.505741 |
| Vertical gap (%) | 4.485582 | 4.401711 | 4.586106 |
| Real CO2 (Mt, calibrated) | 23.387469 | 23.454357 | 23.367309 |
| Ideal CO2 (Mt, calibrated) | 20.914511 | 20.967020 | 20.871810 |

The weighted sample levels therefore differ from the exact total gap by
-0.139799 and -0.083252 percentage points. Reconstruction closure and sampling
error are different checks: exact reconstruction of the sampled flights does
not make the expanded sample equal the full population.

The third run was already in progress when the strict closure guard was added,
so it completed under the preceding in-memory code. Its audit found a maximum
hybrid difference of **131.516952 kg**, while ideal reconstruction was exact.
This is the already measured ERA5 23:00 boundary correction in `KNOWN-ISSUES.md`
section 4, not residual cache-order drift. The weighted hybrid relative change
is only `3.808233639546188e-8`, but small aggregate size does not satisfy a
reference-identity requirement.

The final runner was then exercised on that real case in a private, explicitly
truncated smoke fixture. It exited **1** with `nominal baseline mismatch`:
stored hybrid 80,000.870552 kg, recomputed 80,132.387504 kg. No output JSON was
written. The fixture skipped the already completed full-manifest checksum pass
only to test this numerical guard; it was not an accepted sample result.
The third aggregate is retained as **rejected diagnostic evidence**, not used
to form a three-replication sensitivity range. No tolerance was widened, no
flight was removed and no historical extrapolation was silently restored.

## Limits and next useful work

The finite-difference results are conditional on the historical model,
aircraft mapping, calibration, wind inputs and accepted population. In
particular:

* parameter distributions and correlations are not established;
* one-factor scenarios do not estimate interactions and cannot be added to
  manufacture a total uncertainty interval;
* each ground-fuel share is held fixed while load and reserve vary;
* anchoring stored-track fuel removes nominal thinning drift, but does not
  establish that its parameter response equals the unavailable native trace;
* a ground-speed alternative diagnoses a modelling convention, not the true
  airspeed error or a correction to apply;
* exact nominal closure proves reconstruction, not external validity;
* the rejected and upstream-unobserved populations remain outside this test.

Before another accepted replication, the reference policy needs an explicit
decision. Either reproduce the historical one-day wind convention in a
strictly isolated replay, or define a separately named corrected-wind nominal
and report its difference from the release before measuring sensitivities
around it. The latter avoids reviving a known interpolation defect, but answers
a slightly different question. Neither is implemented here and neither permits
silently accepting an arbitrary nominal mismatch. The current default remains
strict and rejects the observed incompatible case.

After that decision, a useful independent lab step is a better-balanced
sensitivity sample: retain
rare-cell coverage but allocate more observations to the large cells carrying
most of the expansion weight, then rerun the unstable scenarios. Keep this
audit as the exploratory predecessor rather than replacing its numbers.
Wingbits/OpenSky access is still needed for the separate held-out trajectory
validation; it is not a prerequisite for that sampling work.

The user's scope restriction excludes a replacement cruise optimiser and a
full-release regeneration here. Therefore this audit measures sensitivity
around the **historical** reference, not around a potentially better structural
model. No assertion is made about the headline effect of such a replacement.
Privacy, descriptive wording and the prohibition on unsupported probability
intervals did not prevent the checks reported here; they restrict what may be
claimed or published from them.
